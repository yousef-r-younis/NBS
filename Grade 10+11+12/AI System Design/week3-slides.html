Peer Feedback

How to Give Useful Feedback

After each team reads their answers, the class gives structured feedback in three parts.

⭐

What worked

One section that was specific, clear, and well-argued. Not "it was good" — name the section and say why it worked: "Your limitations section was strong because you gave two concrete failure scenarios with real consequences."

🔍

One challenge

Something you weren't convinced by, framed as a question: "How would your system handle inputs in Lebanese dialect?" or "I wasn't sure what bias type you were referring to — can you name it specifically?"

💡

One suggestion

Something concrete they can fix before submitting: "Add a specific mitigation strategy to your bias section" or "Your real-world example needs to compare your system to the existing one — not just name it."

🚫 What NOT to say
  • "It was really good / amazing" — what specifically worked?
  • "I didn't understand anything" — which section specifically?
  • "Your idea is bad" — challenge the content, not the concept
Teacher: Model the feedback yourself after the first team. Show them what specific and useful looks like in practice.
Watch Out For

Most Common Weaknesses
in Draft Answers

❌ "We'll use good data"

Not a bias strategy. Name the bias type — historical, sampling, label, feedback loop — and describe a specific mitigation. "Good data" tells a judge nothing.

❌ "Our system is safe"

Every AI system can cause harm. Give a real harm scenario and a real safeguard. Pretending there are no risks loses points immediately.

❌ "Sometimes it might be wrong"

Not a failure case. Format it properly: user does X → system outputs Y → consequence is Z. Two of these. Specific. Real.

❌ "We use AI / machine learning"

Name the ML type. Classification? Regression? Recommendation? NLP? Computer vision? Not naming it costs you a full rubric criteria point.

❌ "Google does something similar"

Not enough. Name the specific system, then explain how yours differs or improves on it. Comparison is the whole point of the real-world examples section.

❌ Bullet points in any answer

Your answers go directly onto the poster as prose. Bullet points break the layout. Every answer must be written in full sentences — 3 to 5 of them.

Teacher: After the read-alouds, reference whichever of these you heard most. Don't name teams — make it general.
Important

Writing for a Poster
Is Different

Your answers go directly onto an A3 sheet. That changes how you write.

✅ DO

  • Write in full sentences — no bullet points
  • Keep each section to 3–5 sentences maximum
  • Make every sentence add new information
  • Read it aloud — if it sounds robotic, rewrite it
  • Be specific — names, numbers, real examples

❌ DON'T

  • Use bullet points or numbered lists
  • Write more than 5 sentences per section — it won't fit
  • Start sentences with "In conclusion" or "As we mentioned"
  • Copy text directly from Week 1 or Week 2 worksheets without editing
  • Leave any section blank or with placeholder text
