How LLMs Work

Week 1: Understanding Large Language Models

AI Course • Grades 10-12

What is an LLM?

Large Language Model

  • AI trained on massive amounts of text from the internet
  • Learns patterns in language
  • Predicts what text should come next

💡 Think of it like: Autocomplete on your phone, but incredibly sophisticated

All of these work on the same basic principle: predicting the next token

Tokens: The Building Blocks

LLMs don't read words like we do...

They read tokens

What is a Token?

A token ≈ 4 characters or ¾ of a word

"Hello world"

= 2 tokens

"Unbelievable"

= 3 tokens (un-believ-able)

"I love pizza"

= 4 tokens

Why Do Tokens Matter?

Context Windows

LLMs have token limits — they can only "remember" a certain number of tokens at once

  • ChatGPT can remember ~8,000 to 128,000 tokens (depending on version)
  • After that limit, it forgets the beginning of the conversation
  • This is called a "context window"

Think: Short-term memory for AI 🧠

How Are LLMs Trained?

It's a 3-step process

Let's break it down...

Step 1: Collect Massive Data

1 Gather billions of text examples
  • Books and articles
  • Websites and Wikipedia
  • Reddit, forums, social media
  • Code repositories
  • Academic papers

⚠️ Problem: What if the data contains biases, false information, or harmful content?

Step 2: Learn Patterns

2 Practice predicting billions of times

"The cat sat on the ___"

The AI guesses: mat, floor, chair, table

Gets feedback on which predictions are good

Repeats this billions of times until it gets really good!

Step 3: Fine-tuning

3 Specialize for specific tasks

After basic training, companies fine-tune the model:

  • ChatGPT: fine-tuned to be helpful, harmless, honest
  • Some LLMs: fine-tuned for coding
  • Others: fine-tuned for medical advice
  • Others: fine-tuned for legal research

This makes them better at specific tasks!

Today's Key Takeaways 🎯

1. LLMs are AI trained on massive text data to predict what comes next

2. Tokens are the building blocks (~¾ of a word)

3. Context windows limit how much LLMs can "remember"

4. Training involves: collecting data → learning patterns → fine-tuning

5. Training data quality affects LLM outputs (bias, misinformation)
