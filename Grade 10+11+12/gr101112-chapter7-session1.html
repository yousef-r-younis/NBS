AI Ethics: When Smart Tech Makes Hard Choices

Lesson 1 - Chapter 1

AI Ethics: The study of right and wrong in artificial intelligence

AI systems make decisions that affect real people's lives. AI ethics asks:

  • Is this AI system fair to everyone?
  • Can we trust the AI's decisions?
  • Who is responsible when AI makes mistakes?
  • Does this AI respect people's rights and privacy?

Why it matters:

As AI becomes more powerful, its mistakes become more serious. A biased AI can deny someone a job, a loan, or even their freedom.

The Four Pillars of Ethical AI

  • Fairness - AI should treat all people equally, without discriminating based on race, gender, age, or other characteristics
  • Transparency - People should understand how AI makes decisions; "black box" systems are dangerous
  • Accountability - Someone must be responsible when AI causes harm (developer, company, or user)
  • Privacy - AI should protect people's personal information and not violate their privacy rights

Case Study 1 - What Happened?

The Cambridge Analytica Scandal (2016)

The Facts:

  • 87 million Facebook users had their data harvested without proper consent
  • Data was collected through a personality quiz app called "This Is Your Digital Life"
  • The app collected data not just from quiz-takers, but also from all their Facebook friends
  • Cambridge Analytica was a political consulting firm hired for the 2016 US Presidential election

The Timeline:

  • 2014-2015: Data collection occurred
  • 2018: Whistleblower revealed the scandal
  • Public outcry and investigations followed

How Did They Use AI?

Cambridge Analytica's AI Strategy

  1. Used machine learning to build psychological profiles of millions of voters
  2. AI analyzed: Facebook likes, posts and comments, friend networks, online behavior patterns
  3. Created "psychographic" profiles predicting political beliefs, personality traits, fears and what messages would influence each person
  4. Used these profiles to target specific political ads to specific people

The Power: Micro-targeting allowed them to show different messages to different people, making campaigns highly personalized and persuasive.

What Went Wrong?

The Ethical Violations

  • Consent Issues: Users did not know their data would be used for political campaigns; friends of quiz-takers never consented; Facebook's policies were violated
  • Manipulation: AI was used to exploit people's psychological vulnerabilities with targeted misinformation and divisive content
  • Lack of Transparency: Users had no idea they were being profiled and could not opt out
  • Privacy Invasion: Massive scale of data collection without knowledge; personal information used for unexpected purposes

Consequences

Legal Consequences:

  • Facebook fined $5 billion by the FTC
  • Cambridge Analytica shut down in 2018
  • Multiple investigations and lawsuits followed

Regulatory Response:

  • Accelerated passage and enforcement of data privacy laws (GDPR, CCPA)
  • New requirements for political ad transparency and data handling

Public Impact:

  • Massive loss of trust in social media platforms
  • Increased public awareness of data privacy

How Facial Recognition Works

The Process:

  1. Detection - Camera identifies a face in an image
  2. Analysis - AI measures facial features and creates a unique "faceprint"
  3. Comparison - Faceprint is compared against a database
  4. Match - System returns potential matches with confidence scores

How it uses AI: Trained on millions of face images using deep learning to learn facial patterns.

Real-World Uses of Facial Recognition

  • Law Enforcement: Identify suspects from footage; scan crowds
  • Airports & Border Control: Identity verification at checkpoints and gates
  • Schools: Track attendance and entry
  • Private Companies: Phone unlocking, retail analytics, office security

Major Issues with Facial Recognition

  • Racial Bias: Higher error rates for Black, Asian people and women due to skewed training data
  • False Matches: Wrong arrests (e.g., Robert Williams) and wrongful detentions
  • Privacy Invasion: Constant surveillance without consent
  • Misuse Potential: Tracking protesters, stalking, discrimination

Bans and Restrictions

US Cities That Banned It:

  • San Francisco (2019)
  • Boston, Portland, Oakland and others

Why they banned it: Concerns about civil liberties, bias, and lack of transparency

Private Sector: Microsoft, Amazon, IBM paused sales to police

What Are Deepfakes?

Deepfakes are realistic fake videos, images, or audio created using AI (often GANs) that can make it look like someone said or did something they didn't.

How they're made: Train AI on many images/videos, learn expressions and voice, then generate new media.

The Dangers of Deepfakes

  • Misinformation: Fake political videos influencing elections (e.g., falsified clips targeting leaders)
  • Financial Fraud: Deepfake voices impersonating executives for fraud
  • Harassment: Revenge porn and non-consensual explicit content
  • Erosion of Trust: People doubt real evidence

Detection and Solutions

Detection Methods: AI detectors, look for glitches (blinking, lighting inconsistencies), audio-visual sync issues

Provenance: Digital signatures, watermarking, blockchain to verify originals

Legal Responses: Laws criminalizing malicious deepfakes; platform detection tools

Who's Responsible?

  • AI Developer/Programmer: Design, training, testing
  • Company: Deployment decisions, monitoring, fixes
  • End User: Misuse or reliance on AI
  • Government/Regulators: Create and enforce safety standards

The reality: responsibility is often shared among multiple parties.

The Trolley Problem for AI

Self-driving car scenario: the AI may have to choose between harming passengers or pedestrians. Ethical questions: prioritize passengers, save the most lives, consider ages? There is no universally agreed answer.

Balancing Innovation vs. Safety

Innovation: AI can solve big problems but over-regulation may slow benefits.

Safety: Harm from deployed AI can be immediate and irreversible; testing and regulation are necessary.

Finding Balance: Industry standards, pre-release safety testing, rapid response mechanisms, and international cooperation.

Key Takeaways

  • Four ethical principles: Fairness, Transparency, Accountability, Privacy
  • Cambridge Analytica: 87 million users, $5 billion fine, political profiling
  • Facial recognition: racial bias, false matches, bans in several cities
  • Deepfakes: dangers include misinformation, fraud, harassment
  • Trolley problem: ethical dilemmas in self-driving cars
