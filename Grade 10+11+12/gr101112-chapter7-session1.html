AI Ethics: When Smart Tech Makes Hard Choices

Session 1: AI Ethics & Real-World Cases

What is AI Ethics?

Definition: Principles guiding design, deployment and use of AI so it is fair, safe, transparent and respects privacy.

Why it matters:

  • Decisions by AI affect people's lives — hiring, loans, policing, elections

Four Key Ethical Principles

  • Fairness — avoid discrimination
  • Transparency — explain how decisions are made
  • Accountability — who is responsible?
  • Privacy — protect personal data

Case Study: Cambridge Analytica — What Happened?

Facebook data on ~87 million users was harvested and used around the 2016 election.

How Did They Use AI?

Personality profiling from likes and activity; targeted political ads using predictive models.

What Went Wrong?

Consent issues, opaque data collection, manipulation of voter opinions.

Consequences

  • Lawsuits and investigations
  • Regulatory pressure and fines (Facebook fined billions)
  • Public trust erosion

Facial Recognition: How It Works (Short)

Systems map faces to numeric features and compare against databases to identify people.

Real-World Uses

  • Law enforcement
  • Airports and security
  • School safety and attendance

Problems with Facial Recognition

Racial bias, higher false matches for some groups, privacy invasion, mistaken arrests.

Bans and Restrictions

  • San Francisco and other cities restricted police use
  • EU considering strong limits and use-case rules

Deepfakes: What Are They?

AI-generated videos or audio that convincingly impersonate people (celebrities, politicians).

Dangers of Deepfakes

  • Misinformation and political manipulation
  • Fraud, harassment, reputational damage

Detection & Solutions

Tools: AI detectors, provenance (digital signatures), media literacy, watermarking generated content.

Who's Responsible?

  • Developers — design safe models
  • Companies — deploy responsibly and monitor
  • Users — use ethically
  • Governments — create and enforce rules

The Trolley Problem for AI

Example: self-driving car must choose between two harmful outcomes — who decides the trade-off?

Balancing Innovation vs. Safety

Innovation brings benefits but risks must be managed through testing, red-teaming, and regulation.
