What is NLP?

Natural Language Processing — making sense of human language. NLP enables computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language (text and speech).

"Natural Language" means: the way humans actually communicate — not programming languages; it is full of ambiguity, slang, sarcasm and context.

The Goal: computers that can read and understand text, have conversations, translate languages, and write like humans.

NLP in Your Daily Life

  • Voice Assistants: Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant.
  • Translation: Google Translate, real-time captions.
  • Chatbots: ChatGPT, customer service bots, AI tutors.
  • Autocorrect & Suggestions: phone suggestions, Gmail completion.
  • Search Engines & Moderation: smarter search and content filtering.

The Challenge — Ambiguity

Language confuses computers because words have multiple meanings, order matters, and context changes meaning.

  • Example: "I saw her duck" — bird or action?
  • Word order: "The dog bit the man" ≠ "The man bit the dog"
  • Context: "That movie was sick!" could be positive slang.

Sarcasm and Idioms

Sarcasm and idioms require cultural and contextual understanding that AI lacks natively.

  • "Oh great, another test." — sarcasm flips literal meaning.
  • Idioms: "raining cats and dogs", "break a leg" — not literal.
  • Different regions and groups use different slang and expressions.

More Ambiguity Examples

  • Referential: "The chicken is ready to eat" — who is eating?
  • Structural: "I saw the man with the telescope" — who has the telescope?
  • Pronouns: "The trophy doesn't fit in the suitcase because it's too big" — which is big?

What are LLMs?

Large Language Models are AI systems trained on massive text data to understand and generate human-like language.

  • "Large" = billions of words and hundreds of billions of parameters.
  • They learn patterns and predict likely continuations of text.
  • Examples: GPT-3.5, GPT-4, PaLM, LLaMA.

Tokens Explained

Models break text into tokens (words, parts of words, punctuation). Tokenization helps handle new words and manage vocabulary size.

Why it matters: token limits constrain how much text the model can process at once.

How LLMs Generate Text

They predict the next token repeatedly. Given "The capital of France is", the model assigns probabilities (e.g., "Paris" high) and selects tokens until stopping.

Temperature: controls randomness — low = deterministic, high = creative.

Context Windows

The context window is how many tokens the model can 'remember' simultaneously. Smaller windows mean long documents must be chunked or summarized.

  • GPT-3.5: ~4,096 tokens
  • GPT-4: 8,192 tokens (or 32,768 for extended)

Transformers — The Breakthrough

Transformers (2017) process entire inputs at once and use attention to capture relationships between distant words, enabling powerful models like GPT.

Attention Mechanism (Simplified)

Attention scores how much each word should 'pay attention' to others, helping resolve pronouns and long-range dependencies.

Example: In "The animal didn't cross the street because IT was too tired", attention links "IT" to "animal".

Attention — Visual Example

Attention scores show which words influence the interpretation of pronouns like "it" or "her". Transformers use multiple attention heads for different relationships.

Applications — Chatbots

  • Customer service: answer FAQs, escalate to humans
  • Personal assistants: reminders, smart home control
  • Educational chatbots: AI tutors and practice partners
  • Therapy/companions: support but not replacements for professionals

Applications — Translation

Neural machine translation learns mappings between languages and preserves context much better than older rule-based systems. Works best for common language pairs.

Applications — Content Generation

  • Essays, articles, creative writing
  • Code generation (GitHub Copilot)
  • Business writing, summaries, marketing copy

Concerns: academic integrity, job impact, quality, and copyright questions.

Limitations — No True Understanding

LLMs are sophisticated pattern matchers, not conscious reasoners. They can be confidently wrong and struggle with novel physical reasoning.

Limitations — Hallucinations & Bias

  • Hallucinations: making up facts or citations
  • Bias: reproduced from training data (gender, race, cultural)
  • Mitigations: data curation, RLHF, safety testing — ongoing work

Small Group Activity (6 mins)

In groups of 3–4, pick ambiguous sentences and for each:

  1. List possible meanings
  2. Explain why AI finds it hard
  3. Suggest how AI could resolve the ambiguity

Examples: "Time flies like an arrow", "The old man the boats", "I saw her duck".
