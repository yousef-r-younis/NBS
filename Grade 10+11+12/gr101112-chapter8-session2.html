Misinformation & Media Literacy 📺🔍

How to Spot Fake News & AI-Generated Content

Session 8: Critical Thinking in the Age of AI

The Crisis: Information Warfare 🚨

We're facing an unprecedented challenge: it's now possible to create convincing fake content faster than we can verify real content.

Examples:

  • Deepfake videos: Of politicians saying things they never said
  • AI text: Articles that read like real news but are completely fabricated
  • Manipulated images: Photos edited to mislead (AI makes this trivial)
  • Fake studies: AI generates fake research papers

Impact: How do we know what's real anymore?

Fake News: How to Spot It 🚩

RED FLAGS:

  • Extreme headline: "This ONE WEIRD TRICK doctors don't want you to know!"
  • No author/date: Legit news shows who wrote it and when
  • Loaded language: "Sheeple believe X" or "Only smart people know Y"
  • No sources: "Studies show..." (which studies? link them!)
  • Emotional appeals: Designed to make you angry/scared, not think
  • Ads everywhere: Low-quality sites make money from clicks, not truth

CHECK THE SOURCE:

  1. Go to the website's About page
  2. Who owns it? (Owner info should be clear)
  3. What's their track record? (Google them)
  4. Are they registered as news or opinion?

Fact-Check Like a Pro ✅

Trusted Fact-Checking Sites:

  • Snopes.com: Checks viral claims and debunks myths
  • FactCheck.org: University of Pennsylvania's non-partisan fact-checker
  • PolitiFact.com: Checks political claims (shows reliability)
  • Google Fact Check: Search "[claim] fact check"

Before Sharing:

  1. Find the original source (not a screenshot)
  2. Check 3 fact-checking sites
  3. If it's not verified, don't share it
  4. Check publication date (old news is often misleading when shared now)

AI-Generated Images: How to Spot Them 🖼️

TELLTALE SIGNS OF AI ART:

  • Hands: AI makes weird hands (wrong number of fingers, unnatural positions)
  • Text: Words are gibberish or misspelled (AI struggles with text)
  • Details: Small details are blurry or wrong (teeth, eyes, symmetry)
  • Reflections: Don't make sense or are missing
  • Patterns: Backgrounds repeat strangely
  • Physics: Objects defy gravity or don't interact realistically

GOOD NEWS:

AI images still have tells. Bad news: getting better every month. Check hands and text first.

Deepfake Videos: How to Spot Them 🎬

SIGNS OF DEEPFAKES:

  • Eyes: Don't blink properly or don't track with head movement
  • Mouth: Sometimes doesn't sync with audio
  • Face shape: Changes slightly between shots
  • Lighting: Doesn't match environment
  • Hair: Moves unnaturally
  • Shadows: Don't align with light source

STRATEGY:

  1. Check the source (who created this?)
  2. Look up if fact-checkers have analyzed it
  3. Watch it on slow motion (look for glitches)
  4. Check other sources for the event (if real, it would be elsewhere)

Reverse Image Search 🔎

The Power Tool for Verification:

Google Reverse Image Search: Upload a photo to find where it's from

How to use:

  1. Go to google.com/images
  2. Click camera icon
  3. Upload image or paste URL
  4. Google shows you everywhere that image appears

Why it matters:

  • Fake claims: Image is from 2020, but posted as 2024
  • Wrong context: Photo from Africa labeled as Haiti
  • AI detection: If no search results, might be AI-generated

Misleading Statistics 📊

How statistics lie:

  • "90% agree" (90% of 10 people surveyed? or 10,000?)
  • Correlation ≠ causation: "Ice cream sales correlate with drowning" (both peak in summer)
  • Cherry-picked data: "Stocks up 50% this year!" (only up 5% overall, but one sector boomed)
  • Misleading graphs: Y-axis doesn't start at 0 (exaggerates small changes)

QUESTIONS TO ASK:

  • How many people were surveyed?
  • Who paid for this study? (Follow the money)
  • What's the full dataset? (Not cherry-picked?)
  • Have other studies confirmed this?

Be Skeptical of Consensus ⚠️

Three Types of Claims:

  • SCIENTIFIC CONSENSUS: Supported by multiple peer-reviewed studies (example: vaccines work, climate change is real)
  • DEBATE: Experts disagree, cite sources (political positions, economic policy)
  • FRINGE: Few/no credible sources support it (flat earth, vaccines cause autism debunked)

Red flags for MISINFORMATION:

  • Contradicts scientific consensus without new evidence
  • Relies on appeals to authority (influencer says it)
  • No peer-reviewed research
  • Attacks messengers instead of addressing arguments

Student Activity: Media Audit 🔍📰

Analyze 3 pieces of content:

  1. Find 1 news article (any topic)
  2. Find 1 viral social media post (TikTok, Twitter, Instagram)
  3. Find 1 image/video you suspect might be AI-generated

For each, analyze:

  • Source: Who created it? Is it reputable?
  • Evidence: Are claims supported with links/sources?
  • Red flags: Any signs of misinformation?
  • Verification: Check fact-checker sites. Is it accurate?
  • AI?: Does it show signs of AI generation?

Deliverable: Written analysis of 3 pieces (fact vs misinformation assessment)

The Golden Rule of Media 🏆

BEFORE YOU BELIEVE OR SHARE:

  • Check the source (credible outlet?)
  • Look for evidence (sources, links, data)
  • Check other outlets (are they reporting same thing?)
  • Use fact-checkers (Snopes, FactCheck)
  • Reverse image search (especially for photos)
  • Look for red flags (emotional language, urgency, sketchy domain)

If unsure: Don't share it. It's better to stay silent than spread misinformation.

What We Learned 🎓

  • Fake news: Check source, author, date, sources, and red flags
  • Fact-checking: Use Snopes, FactCheck, PolitiFact, Google Fact Check
  • AI images: Look at hands, text, details, reflections for tells
  • Deepfakes: Check eyes, mouth sync, lighting, source
  • Reverse image search: Find context of photos
  • Statistics: Ask sample size, who paid, full data, other studies
  • Critical thinking: Don't assume, verify everything
