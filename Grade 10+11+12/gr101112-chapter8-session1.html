AI Ethics 🤔⚖️

Hard Questions. No Easy Answers.

Session 7: Real-World Dilemmas

Why Ethics Matter 🎯

AI is already making life-or-death decisions. It's not enough to understand how AI works—you need to understand the moral questions it raises.

AI Makes These Decisions:

  • Who gets loan approval (financial discrimination)
  • Who gets parole (criminal justice bias)
  • Who gets hired (job discrimination)
  • Who lives or dies (autonomous vehicles)

And you have a responsibility to think about it.

Case Study 1: Self-Driving Cars 🚗

The Trolley Problem (AI Version):

Self-driving car detects unavoidable crash. It can either:

  • A) Hit 5 pedestrians (kill 5 people)
  • B) Swerve left and hit 1 person in the car (kill driver)

The Question:

How should the AI be programmed?

Why It Matters:

  • This is happening right now (Tesla, Waymo)
  • Engineers have to decide
  • If they choose A, are they liable for those deaths?
  • If they choose B, would you buy it knowing it sacrifices you?

There is no universally "right" answer. Different cultures have different values.

Case Study 2: Facial Recognition 🔍

The Technology:

AI can identify anyone in a crowd. Governments use it to:

  • Find wanted criminals
  • Monitor borders
  • But also: Track protesters, surveillance states

The Ethical Problems:

  • Bias: AI systems misidentify Black people 34% more than white people
  • Privacy: You never consented to being surveilled
  • Chilling effect: People avoid protests if they know they're being watched
  • Police use: Some police departments use it, some ban it

The Question:

Is the security benefit worth the loss of privacy and freedom?

Case Study 3: Job Automation ⚙️

The Reality:

AI can do many jobs better/cheaper than humans. What happens to those people?

Examples:

  • Radiologists: AI reads X-rays better than humans. Should doctors be retrained?
  • Truck drivers: Self-driving trucks coming. 3.5 million job losses in US alone.
  • Customer service: Chatbots replace phone reps

The Ethical Questions:

  • Does society have responsibility to retrain displaced workers?
  • Should companies that profit from automation pay into retraining?
  • What about people who can't retrain (age, health)?
  • Is maximizing profit worth destroying jobs?

Case Study 4: Deepfakes 🎬

What Are They?

AI-generated videos of people (often famous people) saying/doing things they never said/did.

Real Examples:

  • Deepfake of Tom Cruise doing things he never did
  • Deepfake "nude" videos of celebrities
  • Politicians' speeches manipulated to say different things

The Ethical Problem:

  • Non-consensual: People used without permission
  • Harm: Deepfake nudes harm women most
  • Truth: Deepfakes destroy ability to trust video evidence
  • Speech: If you can fake anyone saying anything, free speech breaks

Question: How do we balance free speech with protecting people from harm?

Case Study 5: AI Content & Ownership 📝

The Problem:

AI trained on artists' work without permission. AI now creates art "like" them.

Questions:

  • Copyright: Did companies break the law by using artists' work?
  • Fair use: Is AI training fair use (like humans learning by studying)?
  • Compensation: Should artists be paid when AI is trained on their work?
  • AI-generated content: Who owns it? The person who prompted it? The company?

Reality:

  • This is being litigated RIGHT NOW
  • Courts are still deciding (2024-2025)
  • Different countries have different rules

AI Bias: The Hidden Problem 🎯

Example: Hiring AI

Amazon built AI to screen resumes. It was biased against women.

Why?

Trained on historical hiring data (where men dominated tech). AI learned the bias.

Real Consequences:

  • Qualified women rejected by AI
  • Discrimination hidden in code
  • Harder to catch than human discrimination

The Question:

How do we ensure AI is fair when human data contains bias?

Framework for Ethical Decision Making 🔑

When facing an ethical question, ask:

  1. What's the impact? Who is helped? Who is harmed?
  2. Is it fair? Does it treat everyone equally or are some disadvantaged?
  3. Is it legal? What do current laws say? (Laws aren't always ethical, but useful guide)
  4. What are the alternatives? Could we achieve same goal in a less harmful way?
  5. Long-term effects? What happens in 5-10 years if this becomes standard?
  6. Would I want this done to me? (Golden rule)

Student Activity: Ethics Debate 🗣️

Group Debate Activity:

  1. Split into teams (8 people, 4 per side)
  2. Pick a case study (self-driving cars, facial recognition, etc.)
  3. One team argues FOR the AI use (pros)
  4. One team argues AGAINST (cons)
  5. Each side presents 5 points with logic/evidence
  6. Debate 15 minutes with rebuttal time
  7. Class votes on most compelling argument

Deliverable: Written position paper defending your ethical stance (no "right answer")

Your Responsibility:

Think about ethics. Ask questions. Challenge assumptions. Society needs ethical thinkers.

What We Learned 🎓

  • Self-driving cars: Hard choices about whose life matters
  • Facial recognition: Security vs. privacy tradeoff
  • Job automation: Society's responsibility to displaced workers
  • Deepfakes: Non-consent + erosion of truth
  • AI content: Copyright and ownership in AI age
  • AI bias: Historical bias becomes code bias
  • Framework: Ask impact, fairness, legality, alternatives, long-term, golden rule
