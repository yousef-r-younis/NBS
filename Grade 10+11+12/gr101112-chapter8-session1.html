Session 1: Computer Vision Basics

What is Computer Vision?

Teaching computers to understand images and videos — detect objects, classify scenes, and extract information.

Why it matters:

  • Face unlock and security
  • Medical imaging for diagnosis
  • Self-driving car perception

Why It Matters

  • Face unlock and security
  • Medical imaging for diagnosis
  • Self-driving car perception

Images Are Numbers

Each pixel has RGB values (0–255). Models process arrays of numbers to learn patterns.

Visual Example

Imagine a small image broken into a grid of pixels — AI learns to detect patterns of colors and edges.

The Challenge

Millions of pixels and variations (lighting, angle, occlusion) — how to find useful patterns?

What is a CNN?

Convolutional Neural Network: specialized for images; detects edges, shapes, and higher-level features through layers.

How a CNN Works (Simplified)

  • Early layers: detect edges and textures
  • Middle layers: combine edges into shapes
  • Late layers: detect objects like a cat or sign

Visual Progression

Edges → shapes → object parts → whole object (e.g., eye, nose → face).

Why CNNs Are Powerful

They learn hierarchical features automatically from data and generalize well when trained on large datasets.

Applications

  • Object detection for autonomous vehicles
  • Facial recognition (phones, airports)
  • Medical imaging (tumor detection)

Limitations

  • Adversarial examples can fool models
  • Context problems — may not understand scene
  • Needs massive labeled data

Ethical Issues

Surveillance concerns and bias in face recognition leading to unequal error rates for different groups.
