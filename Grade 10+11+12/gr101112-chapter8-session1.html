How AI Sees the World

Lesson 4 - Chapter 2

What is Computer Vision?

Computer vision trains computers to interpret visual information — images and videos — so they can recognize objects, locations, actions, and scenes.

Why Computer Vision Matters

  • Medical diagnosis (X-rays, MRIs)
  • Self-driving cars (perception and safety)
  • Face unlock and security
  • Agriculture and manufacturing automation

How Computers See Images

Images are grids of pixels; each pixel has RGB values (0–255). A 1000×1000 image is millions of numbers the model must process.

Visual Example - Pixels

Small 8×8 low-res images can be shown as grids of RGB values. AI learns that specific patterns of numbers correspond to objects like a smiley face.

The Challenge

Variation: lighting, angle, occlusion, background changes, and object diversity make recognition difficult.

What is a CNN?

Convolutional Neural Network: scans images with filters to find local patterns (edges, textures) and builds up to higher-level features.

How CNNs Work - Simplified

  1. Layer 1: detect edges
  2. Layer 2: combine edges into shapes
  3. Layer 3: detect object parts
  4. Layer 4: recognize whole object

Visual Progression

Input image → edge maps → shape maps → feature maps → final classification (e.g., CAT with confidence).

Why CNNs Are Powerful

  • Automatic feature learning
  • Transfer learning reuse
  • Robust to some variations
  • Scalable to large images and datasets

Application - Object Detection

Object detection finds WHAT objects are in an image and WHERE they are (bounding boxes + labels). Used in self-driving cars, security, retail analytics.

Application - Facial Recognition

Steps: face detection → face analysis (faceprint) → matching against database. Uses include device unlocking, airport security, and tagging.

Application - Medical Imaging

AI assists radiologists by detecting fractures, tumors, and disease patterns quickly and consistently, increasing diagnostic speed and access.

Limitation - Adversarial Examples

CNNs can be fooled by small pixel perturbations or stickers causing misclassification, posing risk to safety-critical systems.

Limitation - Context Problems

Computer vision detects objects but often fails to understand relationships and scene context (e.g., person eating banana vs. holding banana).

Limitation - Massive Data Needs

Training high-performance models often requires millions of labeled images (ImageNet: 14M). Collecting and labeling data is costly.

Ethical Issue - Surveillance

Widespread cameras + AI enables tracking, behavioral analysis, and social control—undermining privacy and civil liberties.

Ethical Issue - Bias in Facial Recognition

Research shows higher error rates for women and people of color; consequences include false arrests, denied services, and disproportionate surveillance.

Pair Activity

Predict CNN features for 5 images (bicycle, pizza, face, tree, car): early layers detect edges, middle layers detect parts, final layers classify object.

Key Exam Points

  • Images = pixels (RGB)
  • CNN layers: edges → shapes → parts → objects
  • ImageNet: 14 million images
  • Limitations: adversarial attacks, context, data hunger
