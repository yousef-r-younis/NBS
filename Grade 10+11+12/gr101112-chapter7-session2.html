Cybersecurity 🔐

Protecting Yourself Online

Session 5: Staying Safe in the Digital World

You're a Target 🎯

Hackers and scammers target students because they think you're easy targets. But with knowledge, you can protect yourself.

Real Threats:

  • Identity theft: Criminals steal your personal info and open accounts
  • Account takeovers: Hackers access your email, social media, gaming accounts
  • Financial fraud: Criminals steal your money or parents' credit cards
  • Blackmail: Scammers get compromising photos and demand money
  • Data breaches: Companies get hacked and leak your information

Passwords: Your First Defense 🔑

Bad Passwords (NOT these!):

  • "123456" - Too simple, easily guessed
  • "password" - First thing hackers try
  • "YourName2024" - Uses public information
  • "abc123" - Pattern-based

Good Passwords:

  • 12+ characters (length matters most)
  • Mix of uppercase, lowercase, numbers, symbols
  • Example: "BlueMoon$Galaxy92!"
  • Unique for each website (never reuse!)
  • No personal information

Password Managers: Your Secret Weapon 🛡️

What is a Password Manager?

An app that stores all your passwords securely. You only need to remember ONE master password.

Popular Options:

  • Bitwarden: Free, open-source, recommended
  • 1Password: Paid, very secure
  • LastPass: Free version available
  • Apple Keychain: Built into iPhone/Mac

Why use one?

  • Automatically creates strong passwords
  • Automatically fills in passwords (safe from typos)
  • You only remember 1 password
  • Works across all your devices

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) 🔐🔐

What is it?

Two ways of proving it's you: something you know (password) + something you have (phone)

How it works:

  1. You enter your password
  2. App sends a code to your phone
  3. You enter that code
  4. Now you're logged in (hacker needs both password AND phone)

Use 2FA on:

  • Email (most important!)
  • Banking apps
  • Social media
  • Gaming accounts
  • Any account with personal/financial info

Phishing: The #1 Hack Method 🎣

What is Phishing?

Scammers send fake emails/texts pretending to be legitimate companies to steal your info.

Real Example:

Fake Email: "Your Amazon account is locked. Click here to verify your identity."

What happens: You click, enter your password, hackers now own your account

Red Flags:

  • Urgent language: "ACT NOW!" "VERIFY IMMEDIATELY!"
  • Suspicious links (check the URL carefully)
  • Requests for password or personal info (real companies never ask this)
  • Spelling/grammar errors (often translated)
  • Generic greetings: "Dear Customer" not "Dear [Your Name]"

Recognizing Phishing: Practice 🔍

Example 1 - PHISHING:

Subject: URGENT: Your account has been compromised!

"Dear user, click here immediately to secure your account: htp://amazn-verify.com/secure"

PHISHING because: Urgent language, suspicious link, generic greeting, misspelled URL

Example 2 - LEGITIMATE:

Subject: Your Amazon order #12345 has shipped

"Hi Sarah, Your order shipped today. Track it in Your Account → Orders."

LEGITIMATE because: Your name, order number, no clicking required, natural tone

HTTPS vs HTTP: Connection Security 🔒

What's the difference?

  • HTTP: No encryption (anyone can see your data)
  • HTTPS: Encrypted (data is scrambled, only you and site can see)

How to check:

  1. Look at the URL bar
  2. Good: 🔒 https://facebook.com (padlock icon)
  3. Bad: http://bank.com (no padlock, might say "Not Secure")

Rule:

Only enter passwords/credit cards on HTTPS sites (with padlock). HTTP sites anyone on the network can see your data.

Public WiFi Dangers ☠️

Why public WiFi is risky:

  • Anyone on the network can intercept unencrypted data
  • Hackers set up fake WiFi with names like "FREE_AIRPORT_WIFI"
  • You think you're on McDonald's WiFi, actually on a hacker's laptop

What to do on public WiFi:

  • Never: Check email, banking, social media on public WiFi
  • Use VPN: Virtual Private Network encrypts all data (see next slide)
  • Wait: Do sensitive stuff at home on your own WiFi
  • Avoid: Logging into accounts with sensitive info

VPN: Virtual Private Network 🌐🔐

What is a VPN?

Encrypts all your internet traffic so no one (not even WiFi owner) can see what you're doing.

Benefits:

  • Safe on public WiFi
  • Hides your IP address
  • Encrypts all data

Popular VPNs:

  • Proton VPN: Free, secure
  • ExpressVPN: Paid, very fast
  • NordVPN: Paid, popular

When to use:

  • On public WiFi (airport, coffee shop, school)
  • When handling sensitive info remotely

Mobile Security 📱

Protect Your Phone:

  • Lock your phone: Use passcode, not pattern or fingerprint alone
  • Update apps: Security updates patch vulnerabilities
  • Don't install from sketchy sources: Only use App Store/Google Play
  • Limit permissions: Apps shouldn't need access to photos/location
  • Don't jailbreak: Removes security protections
  • Use Find My Device: In case phone is stolen

Social Engineering:

Scammers might call pretending to be tech support. Real tech companies never call you unsolicited. If unsure, hang up and call them back using official number.

  2. Do they have 2FA enabled? (Turn it on if not)
  3. Is your password strong and unique?
  4. Install a password manager

Deliverable: Written report on your security audit

What We Learned 🎓

  • Passwords: 12+ characters, unique per site, use password manager
  • 2FA: Two-factor authentication (password + phone code) is critical
  • Phishing: Urgent emails asking you to verify accounts are usually scams
  • HTTPS: Only enter sensitive info on encrypted connections
  • Public WiFi: Use VPN or avoid sensitive activities
  • Mobile: Lock your phone, only install official apps, watch for social engineering
  • Report threats: If you think you're hacked, immediately change passwords and contact support
