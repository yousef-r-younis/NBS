Your Data: The Fuel for AI

Session 2: Data & Privacy in AI

Why AI Needs Data

AI learns patterns from examples — without data it cannot generalize or make predictions.

Why this matters:

  • Models need examples to learn; more data usually improves performance

Types of Data AI Uses

  • Text (articles, chats)
  • Images and video
  • Behavioral data (clicks, time spent)
  • Personal info (profiles, purchases)

How Companies Collect Your Data

  • Social media (likes, shares, time on page)
  • Shopping & browsing (cookies, purchase history)
  • Smart devices (voice assistants, wearables)
  • Third-party trackers and data brokers

Hidden Data Collection

Data brokers compile profiles from many sources; often users don't know how their data is collected or sold.

What Is Training Data?

Training data are examples an AI learns from — labeled photos, transcripts, logs — the more and better the data, the better the model.

Examples: Image Recognition

Image models may need millions of labeled photos (e.g., ImageNet) to learn robust features.

Where Training Data Comes From

  • Public datasets
  • Scraped web content
  • User contributions and uploaded data

Data Privacy Laws: GDPR

Key rights: consent, right to be forgotten, data portability, and requirements for lawful processing.

Data Privacy Laws: CCPA

California law giving consumers rights over their personal information and requiring disclosure of data use.

Why These Laws Exist

  • To prevent misuse
  • To give people control over their data
  • To reduce harm from breaches and profiling

Real Examples of Data Misuse

  • Target predicting pregnancy and sending ads
  • TikTok data access concerns
  • Large-scale data breaches (Equifax)

Protecting Your Data

Practical steps: strong passwords, 2FA, limit app permissions, use VPNs, and read privacy settings.

The Trade-off

Convenience vs. privacy: many apps trade personalisation for data collection — know what you give up.

THINK-PAIR-SHARE Activity (7 mins)

Question: "What data have you shared today?" Discuss privacy risks and what you would change.

Key Exam Points
