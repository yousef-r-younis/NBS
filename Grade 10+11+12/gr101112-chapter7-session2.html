Your Data: The Fuel for AI

Lesson 2 - Chapter 1

Without Data, AI Cannot Learn

Think of AI like a student:

  • A student needs textbooks and examples to learn
  • The more examples they see, the better they understand
  • AI is the same — it learns from data (examples)

The Rule: More data = Better AI (usually). Examples: GPT-3 trained on ~45 TB of text; image recognition needs millions of photos.

Types of Data AI Uses

  • Text Data - social posts, emails, search queries, books
  • Images & Videos - photos, security footage, medical scans
  • Behavior Data - clicks, time on page, shopping habits
  • Personal Information - name, age, health, political views

Social Media Data Collection

Everything they track: likes, comments, watch time, messages, location, device, and more. The business model: "If you're not paying, you are the product."

Shopping and Browsing

  • Cookies - track across sites
  • Purchase History - predicts future buys
  • Search History - builds a profile of interests
  • Third-Party Trackers - follow you across the web

Smart Devices

  • Voice Assistants - record wake-word audio and uploads
  • Fitness Trackers - heart rate, sleep, location
  • Smart TVs - viewing habits, possible cameras/mics
  • Smart Home - doorbells, thermostats, security cameras

Hidden Data Collection

Data Brokers: Companies like Acxiom and Oracle compile and sell detailed profiles assembled from public records, purchase history, trackers, and more.

What Is Training Data?

Training data = the examples used to teach AI. If training data is biased, the AI will be biased: "Garbage in, garbage out."

Where Training Data Comes From

  • Public Datasets - ImageNet, Common Crawl, Wikipedia
  • Scraped Web Content - Reddit, blogs, news
  • User Contributions - CAPTCHAs and labeled tasks
  • Purchased Data - from brokers

GDPR - European Data Protection

Key Rights: Right to access, right to be forgotten, data portability, right to know. Penalties: up to 4% of global revenue or €20 million.

The Right to Be Forgotten

Under GDPR you can request deletion of your data; companies have 30 days to comply unless legal exceptions apply.

CCPA - California's Data Law

Key rights: right to know, right to delete, right to opt-out of sale, and protection from discrimination for exercising rights.

Why These Laws Exist

To prevent data breaches, misuse, and give people control over personal information. Examples: Equifax, Yahoo, Marriott breaches.

Target Pregnancy Prediction

2012: Target predicted a teen's pregnancy via purchase patterns and sent targeted coupons before family knew — a privacy invasion but legal.

TikTok Data Concerns

Controversy over ByteDance and potential access by Chinese authorities; collects a wide range of device and behavioral data. Government bans on official devices in several countries.

Equifax Breach

2017 breach exposed sensitive data for 147 million people. Consequences: identity theft risk and a $700 million settlement.

Protecting Your Data

  • Use strong, unique passwords and a password manager
  • Enable two-factor authentication
  • Limit app permissions and review privacy settings
  • Use VPN on public Wi‑Fi and privacy browser extensions
  • Exercise GDPR/CCPA rights and opt out where possible

Privacy vs. Convenience

Many services require data to function (maps, personalization). Consider trade-offs and prefer privacy-first alternatives where possible.

Think-Pair-Share Activity

Think: list data you shared today. Pair: discuss surprises and risks. Share: a few pairs report insights.
