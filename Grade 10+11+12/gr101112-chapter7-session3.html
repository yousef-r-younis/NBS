When AI Gets It Wrong

Session 3: Limitations & Failures

AI Is Not Perfect

Common misconception: AI = always right. In reality models make many types of mistakes — technical, ethical, and unexpected.

Categories of Failures

  • Hallucinations — made-up facts
  • Adversarial attacks — intentionally fooled models
  • Bias — unfair outcomes

Hallucinations

AI may confidently generate false or fabricated information (fake citations, invented cases).

Why Hallucinations Happen

Models predict likely text based on patterns — they don't 'understand' facts as humans do.

Adversarial Attacks

Small, often invisible changes to inputs can cause wrong predictions (e.g., misclassifying a stop sign).

Image Example

Adding stickers or modifying pixels can make classifiers err — a safety risk for autonomous systems.

Biased AI Systems

  • Amazon hiring tool discriminated against women
  • COMPAS algorithm had higher false positives for some groups
  • Bias comes from biased training data

Autonomous Vehicle Failures

  • Uber self-driving car fatality (2018)
  • Tesla Autopilot crashes due to edge cases
  • Challenge: AI lacks experience with rare situations

Medical AI Errors

Example: skin cancer detectors underperform on darker skin due to lack of diverse training data.

Why Bias Persists

Training datasets reflect historical and social bias; models amplify those patterns unless actively corrected.

Group Discussion (6 mins)

Given 3 failure scenarios, identify the failure type and propose a mitigation.

Key Points for Exam

  • Understand hallucinations, adversarial attacks, and bias
  • Know examples (Uber, Tesla, Amazon, COMPAS)
  • Be able to suggest mitigation strategies (diverse data, testing, human oversight)
