When AI Gets It Wrong

Lesson 3 - Chapter 1

AI Is Not Perfect

The Common Misconception: "AI is super smart and never makes mistakes."

The Reality: AI makes mistakes all the time; it's only as good as its training data and doesn't truly 'understand' context.

Categories of AI Failures

  • Technical Failures - wrong predictions, crashes
  • Ethical Failures - biased outcomes, privacy violations
  • Unexpected Consequences - systems used in ways not intended

What Are AI Hallucinations?

AI hallucinations: when models generate false information with confidence, e.g., fake citations or invented case names.

Examples from ChatGPT

  • Fake legal cases and citations submitted in briefs
  • False historical facts and made-up scientific papers
  • Confident but incorrect answers in technical and medical domains

Why Hallucinations Happen

Models predict the next word based on patterns in data — they do not have an internal database of verified facts or a concept of truth.

Dangers of Hallucinations

  • Academic integrity issues (fake citations)
  • Misinformation spread
  • Professional liability (lawyers, doctors relying on false outputs)
  • Loss of critical thinking

What Are Adversarial Attacks?

Inputs crafted to deliberately fool AI, such as pixel perturbations in images or hidden audio commands.

Image Adversarial Examples

Example: small invisible changes make a panda image classified as a gibbon, or stickers on stop signs that change detection.

Real-World Risks of Adversarial Attacks

  • Autonomous vehicle failures
  • Security bypasses of facial recognition
  • Spam and malware evasion
  • Financial fraud and military risks

Amazon's Hiring AI Disaster

Amazon trained a resume-sorting model on past resumes and discovered it penalized women — it learned historical hiring bias and was scrapped.

COMPAS - Criminal Justice Bias

Algorithm used for recidivism risk scoring showed higher false positive rates for Black defendants, reflecting biased training data and leading to unfair outcomes.

Why AI Bias Happens

  • Biased training data
  • Sampling and measurement bias
  • Algorithmic design choices
  • Confirmation and feedback loops

Uber Self-Driving Car Death (2018)

Elaine Herzberg was struck and killed when the vehicle misclassified and failed to properly react; human safety driver oversight also failed.

Tesla Autopilot Crashes

Autopilot is not full autonomy; misreadings of road signs, stationary vehicles, and over-reliance by drivers led to multiple crashes and fatalities.

The Edge Case Problem

AI struggles with rare or unusual situations (edge cases) that were not represented in training data — these cause failures with severe consequences.

Skin Cancer Detection - Medical AI Bias

AI trained mostly on light-skinned patients performs worse on darker skin, delaying diagnosis and worsening health disparities.

Medical AI Errors - Continued

Devices like pulse oximeters and datasets biased toward certain demographics cause misdiagnoses and harm for underrepresented groups.

Group Discussion Activity

In groups, analyze one failure scenario (hiring bias, translation error, loan denial). Identify failure type, root cause, and prevention strategies.

Key Points for Exam

  • Define hallucination, adversarial attack, edge case
  • Know examples: Amazon, COMPAS, Uber, Tesla
  • Understand root causes: biased data, edge cases, pattern matching
