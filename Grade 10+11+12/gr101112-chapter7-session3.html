The Analysis Framework

5 Questions to ask about any AI product

Write these down — you'll use them for all 3 case studies

5 Questions for Any AI Product

1. What problem does it solve? (Who's the user?)

2. What type of AI is it using?

3. What data does it need? (Where from?)

4. What works well? (Strengths)

5. What are the limitations or failures?

Case Study 1

ChatGPT

Let's analyze it using our framework...

ChatGPT: Problem & AI Type

1. What problem does it solve?

  • Helps people write, learn, brainstorm, code, get answers quickly
  • Users: Students, professionals, creators, developers

2. What type of AI?

  • Large Language Model (LLM)
  • Text generation based on patterns

ChatGPT: Data

3. What data does it need?

Training Data:

  • Books
  • Websites
  • Articles
  • Code repositories
  • Conversations

User Provides:

  • Prompts/questions

Fine-tuned on:

  • Human feedback (RLHF)

ChatGPT: What Works Well

4. Strengths

  • Great at: writing, explanations, brainstorming, coding help
  • Natural conversational interface
  • Fast and accessible
  • Versatile across many tasks

ChatGPT: Limitations & Failures

5. What are the limitations?

Hallucinations

Makes up facts, fake sources

Knowledge Cutoff

Doesn't know recent events without search

Bias

Can reflect biases from training data

No Understanding

Patterns, not true comprehension

Privacy Concerns

Users might share sensitive info

Let's Discuss 💭

Questions

"Have you experienced any of these limitations?"

"Would you trust ChatGPT to help with medical advice? Why or why not?"

Give students time to share experiences...

Case Study 2

Grammarly

Group Activity

Divide into small groups. Discuss for 5 minutes, then we'll share answers together.

Grammarly: Analysis

1. Problem: Helps people write clearly and correctly. Users: students, professionals, non-native speakers

2. AI Type: NLP (Natural Language Processing) — grammar checking, classification + generation

3. Data: Trained on millions of text samples. User provides: the text they're writing

4. Strengths: Catches grammar/spelling/tone issues, context-aware, integrates everywhere

5. Limitations: Sometimes awkward suggestions, can make writing generic, privacy concerns (reads everything!), doesn't understand creative choices

Let's Discuss 💭

Question

"Do you trust Grammarly with sensitive documents? Why or why not?"

Key point: Grammarly reads everything you type — what are the privacy implications?

Case Study 3

Netflix Recommendations

Individual/Pair Activity

Analyze independently or in pairs for 5 minutes, then we'll discuss as a class.

Netflix: Problem & AI Type

1. What problem does it solve?

  • Helps users find shows/movies they'll enjoy
  • Reduces decision fatigue ("What should I watch?")

2. What type of AI?

  • Recommendation system
  • Machine learning: collaborative filtering + content-based filtering

Netflix: Data

3. What data does it need?

  • Your watch history
  • What you rated/liked
  • What similar users watched
  • Show metadata (genre, actors, director, tags)
  • How long you watched before stopping

Netflix tracks everything about your viewing behavior!

Netflix: What Works Well

4. Strengths

  • Personalized suggestions for each user
  • "Because you watched X, try Y"
  • Discovers hidden gems you wouldn't find alone
  • Improves over time as it learns your taste

Netflix: Limitations & Failures

5. What are the limitations?

Filter Bubble

Keeps recommending similar content → you miss variety

No Context

Doesn't know WHY you watched (maybe you hated it but finished)

Mood Blind

Can't account for your current mood or who you're watching with

Binge-Watching

Promotes excessive watching (ethical concern)

Content Creation

Pressure to create "algorithm-friendly" shows

Let's Discuss 💭

Questions

"Have you noticed Netflix recommending the same types of shows over and over?"

"Is it good or bad that Netflix knows your viewing habits so well?"

Key concept: Filter bubbles can limit your exposure to new things!

Key Lessons 🎯

1. Every AI product solves a specific problem for a specific user

2. All AI needs data — which raises privacy and bias questions

3. Even successful products have limitations and ethical concerns
