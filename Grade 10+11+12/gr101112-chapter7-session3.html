Session 6: Resumes, Cover Letters & Professional Documents

Why This Matters Right Now 🎯

You're entering job/college application season. These skills determine whether you get interviews and acceptances.

Reality:

  • Recruiters spend 6 seconds on your resume
  • Cover letters are your chance to stand out
  • Professional emails are seen before your actual interview
  • These skills are exactly what colleges and employers grade you on

Good news: These are learnable skills. Master them = better opportunities.

Resume 101: Structure 📄

1-Page Rule:

Your resume must fit on ONE page. Everything that doesn't fit: delete it.

Basic Structure:

  1. Header: Your name, phone, email, LinkedIn URL, city
  2. Summary/Objective: 2 lines about who you are (optional for entry-level)
  3. Experience: Jobs, internships, volunteer work (reverse chronological)
  4. Education: School name, graduation year, GPA if 3.5+, relevant awards
  5. Skills: Languages, software, technical skills
  6. Activities: Sports, clubs, leadership (if impressive)

What NOT to include:

  • Photo (unless asked for)
  • Personal info (age, relationship status, health)
  • Salary expectations
  • References (save for when asked)

Resume: Content Rules 💡

Action Verbs (Start each bullet with these):

STRONG: Led, Designed, Implemented, Created, Managed, Analyzed, Developed, Achieved, Increased

WEAK: Worked on, Helped, Did, Responsible for, Participated

Example (BAD):

"Worked at coffee shop. Responsible for cash register and customer service."

Example (GOOD):

"Managed cash register and customer transactions for 50+ daily customers, maintaining 99% accuracy. Trained 3 new employees on POS systems and customer service protocols."

The Rule:

Every bullet should answer: What did you do + what was the impact?

Resume Formatting 🎨

Make it scannable:

  • Font: Arial, Calibri, or Helvetica (12pt for body, 14pt for name)
  • Margins: 0.5 inch all sides
  • Line spacing: 1.15 single spacing
  • Bold/underline: Use for section headers only
  • Colors: Black text only (some companies scan with machines that can't read colors)
  • No tables/graphics: Some systems can't parse them

Files:

  • Name it: FirstName_LastName_Resume.pdf (not "resume.pdf")
  • Save as: PDF (preserves formatting across computers)

Cover Letter: Your Story 📝

What is it?

A 3-4 paragraph letter explaining why you want the job and why you're perfect for it.

Structure:

  1. Opening: Job title you're applying for, where you found the job, why you're interested (1 paragraph)
  2. Why you: 2-3 specific achievements showing you can do the job (2 paragraphs)
  3. Closing: How they can contact you, expressing enthusiasm (1 paragraph)

Tone:

  • Professional but personable
  • Show personality while staying formal
  • One page maximum

Cover Letter: Real Example ✍️

OPENING PARAGRAPH:

"I am writing to express my strong interest in the Marketing Intern position at TechStart Solutions, which I found on LinkedIn. I am excited about this opportunity because I admire your company's innovative approach to digital marketing and believe my skills in social media strategy and data analysis would contribute meaningfully to your team."

WHY YOU (EXAMPLE):

"In my current role as social media coordinator for Student Government, I increased Instagram engagement by 150% in 6 months by analyzing competitor strategies and creating targeted content. Additionally, I completed Google Analytics certification and used data insights to improve our email marketing open rates from 22% to 35%."

Professional Email 📧

Sending Email to an Employer/College:

  • Subject line: Clear and specific (e.g., "Internship Application - Sarah Johnson" NOT "Hi")
  • Greeting: "Dear [Name]" or "Dear [Department]" - look up the person's name
  • Body: 3-4 sentences max. State your purpose. Keep it simple.
  • Sign-off: "Best regards," or "Sincerely,"
  • Signature: Include your full name, phone, LinkedIn URL

GOOD EMAIL:

Subject: Internship Inquiry - Computer Science Student
Body: "Dear Ms. Chen, I am a junior studying computer science at Lincoln High School with strong interests in cybersecurity. I saw your company hiring interns and would love to learn more about the position. I have attached my resume. Thank you for your time. Best regards, Sarah Johnson"

Professional Email Mistakes ❌

DON'T:

  • Use abbreviations: "u" for "you", "ur" for "your"
  • Use excessive punctuation: "Hi!!!!" or "???"
  • Send before proofreading (read it out loud 3 times)
  • Attach files they didn't ask for
  • Write in ALL CAPS (shouting)
  • Use emojis (except rare appropriate contexts)
  • Send from unprofessional email (use firstname.lastname@email.com)

Common Mistakes:

  • "Dear Whom This May Concern" (outdated, try to find a name)
  • Sent on Friday night when busy (send during business hours)
  • Huge attachments (compress PDFs first)

Academic Papers & Formal Essays 📚

Format Requirements (MLA/APA):

  • Font: Times New Roman, 12pt
  • Spacing: Double-spaced throughout
  • Margins: 1 inch all sides
  • Header: Your name, class, date on top
  • Title: Centered, not bold
  • Citations: Required for any sources not your own idea

Key Rules:

  • First sentence hooks the reader
  • Thesis statement in introduction (your main argument)
  • Each paragraph = 1 main idea with supporting evidence
  • Conclusion restates thesis and broader implications
  • Proofread: grammar, spelling, word count requirements

Student Activity: Documents 💼✍️

Create the Following:

  1. Your Resume (1 page):
    • Your real contact info
    • Your actual education/experience
    • Skills (list 5-10 relevant skills)
    • Activities/volunteer work
  2. A Cover Letter (1 page):
    • Pick a real job you're interested in (summer, internship, first job)
    • Research the company
    • Write 3-4 paragraphs explaining why you want it
  3. Professional Email:
    • Write an email to someone at that company/organization
    • Ask about opportunities or request informational interview

Deliverable: Resume + cover letter + email (can be digital or printed)

Pro Tips 🌟

Resume/Cover Letter:

  • Tailor each one: Don't send generic documents. Research the company.
  • Use their language: If posting mentions "data-driven", use that exact phrase
  • Quantify achievements: "Increased by 40%" beats "improved a lot"
  • Get feedback: Have a teacher/parent review before sending
  • Save everything as PDF: Protects formatting

Email:

  • Check ONE more time for typos (especially recipient's name!)
  • Send tests to yourself first (check how it looks)

What We Learned 🎓

  • Resume: 1 page, action verbs, quantified results, professional formatting
  • Cover letter: 3-4 paragraphs showing why you want the job and why you're qualified
  • Professional email: Clear subject line, specific body, proper greeting/closing
  • Academic papers: Proper format, citations, thesis-driven structure
  • Overall: Tailor documents to the job, proofread obsessively, get feedback
